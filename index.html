Who could read?

I'm Ada, and I study history. This page asks how one machine changed the answer.

The metal type above says the same thing, in mirror image. Only the printed page reads the right way round.

Before the press, each book was copied by hand, and most Europeans could not read one. Around 1450 in Mainz, Johannes Gutenberg put movable metal type to work in a press, and the same page could be printed again and again.

Print did not teach anyone to read. It gave more people something to read, and over the following centuries that changed who could take part in an argument. Access to knowledge shapes who gets a voice.

How print spread

  1. c. 1450 Gutenberg's workshop in Mainz starts printing with movable metal type.
  2. c. 1455 The Gutenberg Bible is finished, in Latin. Around 180 copies are made.
  3. 1476 William Caxton sets up England's first press at Westminster, and soon prints Chaucer in English.
  4. 1517 Martin Luther's Ninety-five Theses are reprinted in several cities within months. His later German pamphlets reach readers who have no Latin.
